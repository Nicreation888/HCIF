About HCIF

Investing where opportunity and community meet

The Horizon Community Impact Foundation invests in nonprofit initiatives that expand opportunity, strengthen community well-being, and produce meaningful, measurable benefits.

We value community-informed solutions, responsible stewardship, collaboration, and practical approaches that can be sustained beyond the grant period. Competitive projects show a clear connection among the need, the activities, the people who will benefit, and the results expected.

Eligibility

Is your organization and project a fit?

Check the lead applicant, project location, and intended use of funds before investing time in a Letter of Inquiry.

Eligible lead applicants

Meet one condition

  • A U.S.-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit in good standing
  • A public agency in formal partnership with an eligible 501(c)(3)
  • A community-based organization with an eligible 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor

Project requirements

Demonstrate readiness

  • Primarily benefit a U.S. community or population
  • Respond to a clearly documented community need
  • Show capacity to manage the work, funds, and reporting

Not funded

Common exclusions

Individuals and for-profits; partisan activity or lobbying; religious instruction; endowments or debt retirement; fundraising events or regranting; pre-award costs; and major construction or property acquisition.

Full proposal

Required components

Use headings that correspond to these components. The seven-page limit applies to the narrative, not the listed exclusions and attachments.

01Cover page

Applicant and project details, funding priority, request amount, project period, and authorized representative.

02Executive summary

A concise overview of the need, response, intended participants, request, and expected results—maximum 300 words.

03Organization & capacity

Mission, relevant experience, population served, staff and partner roles, and grant-management capacity.

04Statement of need

Credible evidence of the need, affected population, contributing conditions, service gaps, and urgency.

05Project design

Goals, outcomes, activities, implementation approach, responsibilities, partnerships, and timeline.

06Evaluation plan

Indicators, data sources, collection methods, responsibilities, and use of findings.

07Budget & narrative

A complete project budget with other resources and a clear justification for each cost.

08Sustainability

A realistic plan for continuing benefits, effective practices, partnerships, or essential activities.

09Attachments

Project timeline, logic model, board list, IRS letter or fiscal-sponsor documentation, and specified materials.

Frequently asked questions

Before you begin

Which annual cycle should I use?

Use the next available September 30 deadline. In the COMM 355 simulation, spring and summer students ordinarily use the current-year cycle; fall students ordinarily use the following-year cycle.

May a project address more than one funding priority?

Yes. Select one primary priority and explain where the project has its strongest alignment.

Is a match required?

No. Matching funds are not required. Include other committed or pending resources in the complete project budget.

Can indirect costs be included?

Yes. Administrative or indirect costs may not exceed 10% of the amount requested from HCIF.

Does an LOI invitation guarantee funding?

No. An invitation means the project may advance to the full-proposal stage; it is not a promise of an award.

What if a deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday?

The deadline moves to the next business day. Submissions are due by 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time.
